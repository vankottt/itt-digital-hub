# Commission AI literacy Q&A (selected)

Source: European Commission, “AI literacy questions and answers”.
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers

This is official Commission material. It is not the regulation. Where this Q&A says “sufficient level” and Article 4 as consolidated on 27.07.2026 says “support the development of AI literacy” and “does not require … any specific level of AI literacy of any individual”, prefer the regulation and say that the Q&A may reflect earlier wording.

The extracts below are paraphrased tightly from the Q&A. Open the URL for the full text.

## What Article 4 is about (Q&A)

The Q&A states that providers and deployers should take measures so that staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf have AI literacy, taking into account technical knowledge, experience, education and training, the context of use, and the persons on whom the systems are used.

AI literacy is defined by reference to Article 3(56).

“Other persons” can include people under the organisational remit who are not employees, for example a contractor.

## What the Q&A says is not required

- No obligation to **measure** each employee’s AI knowledge.
- No **certificate** is required. Organisations may keep an internal record of trainings or other initiatives.
- No specific **AI officer** or **governance board** is mandated for Article 4. A DPO is not required to be duplicated as an “AI officer” by Article 4.

## Minimum content the Q&A suggests for a literacy programme

The AI Office says it will not impose a strict template. As a minimum, providers and deployers should:

- ensure a general understanding of AI in the organisation (what it is, how it is used, opportunities and dangers);
- consider their role (provider or deployer);
- consider the risk of the systems they provide or deploy;
- build actions on that analysis, including differences in staff knowledge and the context of use.

Formal classroom training is not the only possible measure. Relying only on reading the vendor’s instructions may be ineffective.

## Direct example in the Q&A (ChatGPT for text)

The Q&A asks whether a company whose employees use ChatGPT for writing advertisement text or translating text needs to comply with Article 4. The Commission answer is **yes, they should be informed about the specific risks, for example hallucination**.

Treat this as official guidance that using a general-purpose chatbot at work is in scope of literacy measures for a deployer. Combine it with the consolidated Article 4 wording (support literacy; no guaranteed individual level).

## High-risk overlap

The Q&A notes that deployers of high-risk systems have a separate duty under Article 26 to ensure staff dealing with the system in practice are sufficiently trained for human oversight. That is not the same as Article 4 for ordinary chatbot drafting.

## Living repository

The Commission points to a living repository of literacy practices. Copying a practice from the repository does not automatically prove compliance.
