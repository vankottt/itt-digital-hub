# Prohibited practices and high-risk classification

Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, consolidated 27.07.2026.
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27

This file is a screening aid. It does not reproduce every exception in Article 5.

## Article 5 - prohibited AI practices (summary of the list)

The following AI practices are prohibited (subject to the detailed wording and exceptions in Article 5):

- subliminal, manipulative or deceptive techniques that materially distort behaviour and cause or are reasonably likely to cause significant harm;
- exploitation of vulnerabilities due to age, disability or a specific social or economic situation, with the same harm threshold;
- generating or manipulating intimate / sexually explicit material of an identifiable person without the required consent;
- social scoring that leads to detrimental treatment in unrelated contexts or that is unjustified or disproportionate;
- risk assessment of a natural person to predict criminal offending based solely on profiling or personality traits (with a stated exception for supporting human assessment already based on objective facts linked to criminal activity);
- untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV to create or expand facial-recognition databases;
- inference of emotions of a natural person in the workplace and in education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons;
- biometric categorisation to infer race, political opinions, trade-union membership, religion or belief, sex life or sexual orientation (with a stated law-enforcement dataset exception);
- ‘real-time’ remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement, unless a listed objective and the Article 5 conditions apply.

Ordinary use of a general-purpose chatbot to draft text is not, by itself, an Article 5 practice. Ask what the system is actually used for.

## Article 6 - high-risk AI systems (extract)

An AI system is high-risk where it is a safety component of a product (or is itself a product) covered by Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I and that product/system must undergo third-party conformity assessment for placing on the market or putting into service.

AI systems that are solely used for non-safety related user assistance, performance optimisation, service efficiency, automation, convenience or quality control shall not qualify as safety components. AI systems whose failure or malfunctioning would endanger health and safety shall qualify as safety components.

In addition, AI systems referred to in Annex III shall be considered to be high-risk.

A provider who considers that an AI system referred to in Annex III is not high-risk shall document its assessment before that system is placed on the market or put into service (Article 6(3) in the consolidated text).

## Article 26 - deployers of high-risk systems (extract)

These duties apply to **deployers of high-risk AI systems**, not automatically to every team using a general-purpose chatbot:

- use the system in accordance with the instructions for use;
- assign human oversight to people with the necessary competence, training, authority and support;
- monitor operation and inform the provider / authority if the use may present a risk;
- keep logs under their control for at least six months unless other law says otherwise;
- before use at the workplace, employers inform workers’ representatives and affected workers.

Do not apply the full Article 26 list to a 7-person firm whose only AI use is drafting with a public chatbot, unless facts show a high-risk system.
