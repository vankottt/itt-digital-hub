# CIT WEBSITE — PRODUCT BRIEF

## 1. Product purpose
Create the public digital presence of the Center for Intelligent Technologies as an autonomous, modern research and applied-innovation identity institutionally anchored at UASG.

## 2. Source-derived conceptual core
The Center is conceived as an interdisciplinary platform connecting education, academic research and applied science around the architecture, engineering, testing and continuous adaptation of social-institutional systems.
Its distinctive logic treats institutions, organizations, policies and markets as designed systems composed of goals, actors, roles, rules, decision points, information flows, incentives, feedback mechanisms and measurable outcomes.
AI is an enabling capability inside this broader systems approach, not the sole identity of the Center.

## 3. Website positioning
The site must communicate, within the first 10 seconds, that CIT is:
- interdisciplinary;
- research-led;
- systems-oriented;
- technologically current;
- institutionally credible;
- oriented toward real-world application.

Working positioning territory:
Human systems × systems engineering × data × intelligent technologies × applied research.

## 4. Primary audiences
- Public institutions and regulators.
- Universities, researchers and students.
- Business and industry partners.
- Potential project and funding partners.
- International research organizations.
- Media and informed public.

## 5. Primary user questions
A visitor should quickly understand:
- What is the Center?
- What problem space does it work in?
- What is distinctive about its methodology?
- What does it research?
- What does it apply in the real world?
- Who is involved?
- How can an institution, university or company work with it?

## 6. Core website jobs
A. Establish credibility without pretending maturity that does not yet exist.
B. Explain a non-obvious intellectual proposition clearly.
C. Show the connection between education, research and applied science.
D. Turn methodology into visible, understandable system diagrams and workflows.
E. Present pilots and future projects as applied research, not marketing case studies.
F. Create clear entry points for collaboration.
G. Provide an architecture that can grow into research programs, publications, demonstrators, education and technology transfer.

## 7. V1 differentiators
- Original systems-engineering visual language.
- Strong methodology section.
- Applied-project template.
- Institutional network presentation.
- Bilingual content.
- Research/insight publishing model ready for growth.

## 8. Non-goals for V1
Do not build:
- user accounts;
- student portal;
- complex CMS unless justified later;
- ecommerce;
- large searchable publication database;
- fake dashboards;
- fake metrics;
- speculative partner or funding claims;
- generic AI chatbot merely for visual effect.

## 9. Content truth rules
Never invent:
- team titles;
- formal roles;
- partners;
- project results;
- funding;
- clients;
- publications;
- metrics.
Use explicit labels such as Working concept, Pilot concept, In development or To be confirmed when necessary.

## 10. Success definition
The V1 succeeds if a serious external visitor can understand the Center, trust its institutional and scientific intent, grasp its systems approach, see at least one concrete applied direction and identify how to engage — without the site appearing either unfinished or artificially inflated.
